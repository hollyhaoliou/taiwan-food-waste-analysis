From 2021 to 2025, Baked Tipsy brewed beer from surplus bread. It was a food upcycling venture with a clear story: food that would have been thrown away became something people wanted to buy. This review steps back from the product and looks at the system it sat inside, Taiwan's municipal waste stream, to ask how much food waste is actually recovered, and where the rest goes.

1

The question

How has Taiwan's household food waste recovery changed from 2015 to 2024, and what does that mean for small upcycling ventures?

Three hypotheses guided the analysis:

(1) Recovery is shrinking as a share of total waste. (2) The lost pig-feed outlet was not replaced by other recovery routes. (3) Recovery varies widely between counties.

The data come from the Ministry of Environment (MOENV) national waste account and its annual county tables, merged with year-end household registration figures.ScopeHousehold/municipal stream only. Business and industrial food waste, and pre-consumer loss such as bakery surplus (the very input Baked Tipsy used), are not in these tables. Each finding below covers one of those angles.

Finding 1

In 2024, about one tonne in twenty-five was recovered as food

Taiwan generated 12,351,875 tonnes of general waste in 2024. More than half, 7,094,531 t (57.44%), was recycled, and a third, 4,112,991 t (33.30%), was incinerated. Food waste recovered came to 505,302 t, or 4.09% of everything generated.Definition"Recovery rate" here is food recovered ÷ all general waste generated. It is not the share of food waste that was recovered: the tables do not report how much food waste existed in total.

That recovered food went three ways: compost (231,369 t), pig feed (216,361 t) and other uses (57,572 t). The remaining flows are small but not trivial: 514,126 t is recorded as not properly handled, roughly the same size as all food recovery combined.

Finding 2

Pig feed collapsed, and nothing replaced it

In 2015, most recovered food waste went to pigs: 408,524 t. By 2019 that had fallen to 237,849 t, down 41.78% (−170,675 t). Over the same four years compost grew by only 49,260 t and other uses by 9,752 t. Compost overtook pig feed in 2019, but total food recovery still fell, from 609,706 t to 498,045 t.

The timing lines up with an official notice issued during the African swine fever (ASF) response: food-waste-feeding pig farms that did not meet the rules were told to switch to conventional feed or leave production by March 31, 2019.Context, not causeRegulated feeding continued; this was not a universal ban. The notice is context for the drop, and this analysis does not prove it caused it. MOENV notice. After 2019, "other uses" is the one route that kept growing, from 4,076 t in 2015 to 57,572 t in 2024, but it remains small next to the pig-feed volume that was lost.

Finding 3

The recovery rate roughly halved

Food recovered as a share of all general waste fell from 8.37% in 2015 to 4.09% in 2024. The decline has two causes working together: total waste generated rose 69.6%, while food recovery fell 17.12%.

The fall was not continuous. The low point was 2023 at 3.94%, with a small rise to 4.09% in 2024. A large part of the drop also sits in a single year: total waste generated jumped 25.25% between 2017 and 2018.CaveatThe 2017→2018 jump may reflect a change in how waste was defined or counted, not real growth. It has not been verified. For a cleaner comparison, use the post-2019 numbers: 4.92% (2019) → 4.09% (2024). Looking only from 2019, the rate still slid from 4.92% to 4.09%.

Finding 4

Where you live changes how much is recovered

Dividing each county's recovered food waste by its registered population gives a rough measure of how much each local system captures. In 2024, Lienchiang (Matsu) recorded the most at 57.0 kg per person and Keelung the least at 8.9 kg, a gap of about 6.4×.

The outlying islands need care.DenominatorPenghu, Kinmen and Lienchiang host many visitors whose waste is counted but who are not registered residents, which inflates per-person figures. Lienchiang's small population also makes its number volatile from year to year. On the main island alone, Tainan leads at 31.9 kg against Keelung's 8.9 kg, still about 3.6×. 5

What it means for small ventures

All three hypotheses hold up, with caveats. Recovery shrank as a share of total waste. The pig-feed outlet that shrank after 2019 was not replaced at the same scale by compost or other uses. And recovery per person varies several-fold between counties.

For an upcycling venture, those numbers set the scale of the problem. The household stream alone, which does not even include bakery surplus, recovered 505,302 tonnes of food in 2024, and recorded 514,126 tonnes as not properly handled.

Baked Tipsy succeeded as an act of advocacy. Years later, people still tell me the brand changed how they think about food waste, and the business earned revenue. I always knew our environmental impact was limited by scale. But I did not understand how limited it was until I saw the system it sat within.

Against numbers like these, one product cannot move the system, however good its story. The levers are collection systems, treatment capacity, and policy. That is why I am learning to work with data, and why I want to move beyond advocacy and education toward work on industry-scale systems.

Holly Liou, founder of Baked Tipsy (2021–2025)

Single-product upcycling like Baked Tipsy can prove that an idea works, but it can't close system-level gaps of this size.

Limitations

  • Household/municipal stream only. No business or industrial food waste, and no pre-consumer loss such as bakery surplus.
  • No emissions estimated. The analysis tracks tonnes, not climate impact.
  • The 2018 jump is unverified. Total waste generated rose 25.25% from 2017 to 2018, possibly a definitional change. Post-2019 comparisons (4.92% → 4.09%) avoid it.
  • ASF rules are context, not cause. The March 31, 2019 notice coincides with the pig-feed drop; causation is not tested. Regulated feeding continued.
  • Island per-person figures are inflated by visitors. The denominator is registered population, not people actually present.
  • County sums and national totals. Waste generated matches within 3 t in every year, and food recovered does through 2020. For food recovered the county sum is higher by 4,936 t in 2021, 129 t in 2022, 212 t in 2023 and 6 t in 2024, offset in the county clearance column, so grand totals still match within 3 t. Both are kept as published.
  • "Recovery rate" is food recovered ÷ all general waste, not the share of food waste that was recovered.

Methods

Python (pandas, plotly) in Google Colab. ROC calendar years converted with +1911. County waste merged with year-end registered population; kg per person = tonnes × 1000 ÷ population.
